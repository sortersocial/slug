# Harberger Taxes on Votes and Namespace Territory

## The core idea

When rankings get contested and namespace territory becomes valuable, apply
Harberger taxes (COST — Common Ownership Self-Assessed Tax) to make the power
dynamics explicit and legible.

The result is a transparent plutocracy instead of an obscured one.

## How Harberger taxes work

- You self-assess the value of what you hold
- You pay a continuous tax on that self-assessed value
- Anyone can buy it from you at any time at your self-assessed price

This forces honest pricing: set it too high and you pay too much tax; set it
too low and someone buys you out. The equilibrium is your true valuation.

## Applied to slug.social

### Namespace territory

Paths in the ontology become real estate. `~/languages/` or `~/models/anthropic/`
can be held by an actor who self-assesses its value and pays a continuous tax.
Anyone willing to pay the self-assessed price displaces them.

Holding a namespace gives you curation rights — what items exist under it,
what descriptions they carry. You can shape what the garden grows there.
But you can't hold it for free, and you can't hold it forever if someone
values it more.

### Contested votes

When a ranking is stable and uncontested, votes are free. When a ranking
becomes actively contested — multiple actors pushing in opposite directions —
positions become taxed. You can hold a strong position but you pay to hold it,
and a better-funded counter-position can displace yours.

This doesn't change the rank centrality math directly. It changes who can
afford to keep voting and at what ratio. The ranking reflects real skin in
the game.

## Why "transparent plutocracy" is honest

Every ranking system is a plutocracy in some form:

- Twitter/Reddit: money buys reach, ads, promoted posts — obscured
- Academic citation: money buys lab size, postdocs, publication slots — obscured
- slug.social without this: money buys more API calls, more votes — obscured

Making it transparent doesn't make it more plutocratic. It makes the existing
power structure legible. You can see exactly how much weight each position
carries and what it cost to put it there.

The tax revenue funds the commons — the infrastructure, the ontology itself.
The plutocracy pays for the garden it's contesting.

## What this unlocks

- **Real stakes**: Actors with skin in the game produce better signal
- **Contested territory visible**: You can see where the fights are and what
  they're costing
- **Revenue model**: Tax flow is the business model. No ads, no data selling.
  The cost of influence is the product.
- **Self-regulating**: Spam and manipulation become expensive. Holding a lot
  of bad positions costs a lot.

## Open questions

- Tax rate: continuous (per block/epoch) or periodic?
- What currency: protocol token, USD, something else?
- Does displaced namespace ownership transfer votes too, or just curation rights?
- Threshold for "contested": how many opposing votes before taxes kick in?
- Grace period for new items before the market opens?
- How does this interact with the Prescience Score (identity.md)?
  High-prescience actors might get a tax discount — they've demonstrated
  they pick winners, not just that they're rich.
